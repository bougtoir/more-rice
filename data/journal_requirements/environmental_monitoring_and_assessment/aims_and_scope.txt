Aims and scope

Environmental Monitoring and Assessment emphasizes technical developments and data arising from environmental monitoring and assessment, the use of scientific principles in the design of monitoring systems at the local, regional and global scales, and the use of monitoring data in assessing the consequences of natural resource management actions and pollution risks to man and the environment.

The journal covers a wide range of pollutants and examines monitoring systems designed to estimate exposure both at the individual and population levels. The journal also focuses on the development of monitoring systems related to the management of various renewable natural resources in, for instance, agriculture, fisheries and forests.

The journal also includes research and monitoring systems that help assess anthropogenic impacts on natural resources and the environment from numerous activities, such as harvesting, development, and land use changes. Geographic information system analyses and remote sensing studies relating such activities to land cover changes that affect, e.g. biodiversity and global climate change are also within the purview of the journal.

Preference is given to studies with new and original environmental field data collected and analyzed by the authors.

Specific areas of interest within the scope:

1.The design and development of new single medium and multimedia monitoring systems, new sampling techniques, optimization of monitoring networks, data handling, quality and assurance procedures.
2. The scientific basis for monitoring: novel scaling methods.
3. The use of biological indicators, dynamic and commitment models where actual field data is used, pollution indices, etc.
4. The development of novel monitoring systems which allow direct or indirect estimates of pollutant exposure to critical receptors.
5. Methods and procedures of pollution risk assessment, relating to sources, pathways of exposure, trends in time and space, anticipatory systems, evaluation of environmental quality and of management practice.
6. Monitoring systems designed to detect the effects of changes in land-use patterns on ecosystem processes.
7. Monitoring of ecological processes
8. Development of analytical chemistry techniques for environmental pollutants

Areas generally out of scope:
1. Landslides, slope stability studies
2. Floods except monitoring the impact of floods on ecosystems processes.
3. Laboratory based toxicology studies
4. Epidemiology studies except where there is a strong link to pollution monitoring 
5. Strictly modelling studies with little field data support
6. Studies on Crop Management
7. Studies on methods to clean up waste streams with no direct relationship to pollutant monitoring at the waste source
8. Economic based studies including green studies
9. Droughts
10. Climatology studies without relation to pollution impacts on climate for example studies on rainfall distribution are out of scope 
11. Ecosystem services studies and predictive models to simulate future scenarios based primarily on economic, management or policy inputs
12. Basic erosion studies
13. Studies on soil classification systems
14. Studies on pesticide efficacy 
15. Indoor pollution studies
16. Occupational health papers
17. Plant disease detection systems
18. Bibliometric studies
19. Agronomic studies i.e. How to produce more corn by adding more nitrogen
20. Case studies of LULC Dynamics and Prediction
