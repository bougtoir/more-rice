# 05 — Dynastic, Administrative, and Practical Networks of James I's Realm

Work Package C. Three *separate* networks. An edge exists only where the specific item moved; family
relations never proxy agronomic transfer.

## (i) Dynastic / diplomatic network (dated edges)

| Edge | Date | Nature | Agronomic item attested? |
|------|------|--------|--------------------------|
| Valencia ↔ Aragon/Catalonia | 1238–1276 | Composite Crown of Aragon (same monarch) | None located in searched sources |
| Valencia ↔ Castile | 1244 (m. Violant/Yolande–Alfonso X) | Dynastic marriage | None located |
| Valencia ↔ France | 1262 (m. Isabella–Philip III); Treaty of Corbeil 1258 | Marriage + treaty | None located |
| Valencia ↔ Sicily/Naples | 1282+ (Sicilian Vespers; post-James) | Dynastic (Peter III–Constance) | Post-period; none located |

**Inference permitted by evidence:** channels existed through which *any* Valencia-centred knowledge *could*
have travelled — court staff, brides' households, ecclesiastical networks. **No searched source attests an
agricultural item moving along any of them.** Stated as "not documented in searched sources" — never
"did not spread."

## (ii) Administrative / landlord / legal network

- **Water institutions (verified transfer, intra-Valencia only):** Islamic irrigation governance (acequia
  hierarchies, water rotation, community water tribunals) was *continued*, not exported — Furs and cartas
  puebla protected existing practice in place. This is D7 *within* Valencia, not transmission *from* it.
- **Legal templates:** repartimento/donation administrative forms are Crown-of-Aragon-wide (Valencia,
  Orihuela, Mallorca share the genre) — administrative templates travelled (D7 for `admin`), crop content
  did not demonstrably follow.
- **Fiscal registers:** no rice-specific fiscal entry located outside Valencia in searched sources.

## (iii) Practical network — peasants, Mudéjars, specialists, merchants, seeds

- **Mudéjar cultivators:** retained in Valencia (documented in repartimiento arrangements); the tacit
  D3/D4 they carried stayed local — the mechanism that *preserved* cultivation also *contained* it:
  knowledge lived in resident communities, not portable manuals.
- **Merchants:** moved `grain` at D1 along Mediterranean routes (rice as imported good in France/England).
- **Seeds/tools/specialists:** no documented movement beyond Valencia located.
- **Migrations:** Christian settlers into Valencia came from Catalonia/Aragon — a reverse edge that imported
  *non-rice* agronomy into a rice zone, reinforcing the local boundary.

## What demonstrably crossed each border (answering spec question 1)

- Valencia→Aragon/Catalonia: **administrative templates** (repartimento genre) only; no crop item attested.
- Valencia→Castile: **commodity grain** (D1) via commerce; dynastic personnel; no cultivation item attested.
- Valencia→France: **commodity grain** (D1); no cultivation item attested.
- Valencia→England/Germany: **commodity grain** (D1) only.
- Iberia (pre-conquest)→Valencia: **knowledge, personnel, water institutions** (D3/D4/D7) — the only
  verified full chain, and it is conquest-inheritance, not diffusion.

## On the James I inference (spec question 4)

His intra-realm and dynastic links show that *political contact* and *a unified legal-administrative space*
were insufficient to transmit cultivation technology: the item that demonstrably moved was the
**administrative form** (repartimento), the item that stayed was the **crop**. This inverts the naïve
"containment by policy" story — diffusion stopped at the practical layer (seeds, cultivators, water works),
not the dynastic one.
