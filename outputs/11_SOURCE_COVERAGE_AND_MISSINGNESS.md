## Source classes able to record diffusion, and their survival

1. **Repartimento/donation registers** — record land + grantees, not crops; the genre's silence is default.
   Survive: Valencia (transcribed Reg. 5; Reg. 6–7 in archive), Orihuela (codex, unaccessed).
2. **Fueros/cartas puebla** — record rights and institutions, not crops. Survive and verified: water clauses.
3. **Fiscal/tax registers** — would record crop-specific dues if they existed; none located for rice outside
   Valencia before 1300 → MIS.
4. **Croniques/chancery** — James I's *Llibre dels fets* covers conquest, not agronomy → NEG for D3–D6.
5. **Merchant/household accounts** — record commodity rice (D1 POS in France/England) but never cultivation.
6. **Agronomic texts** — Ibn al-Awwam untranslated in period (K4 gap, previously established): the *textual*
   channel was genuinely closed.
7. **Archaeobotany** — best independent evidence for D6; available record is thin and geographically uneven
   (Peña-Chocarro for Iberia); no medieval rice macrofossils reported for N. Europe in searched literature.

## Missingness structure

- The *textual* channel is verifiably absent (K4). The *personnel* channel leaves almost no written trace by
  design — D0 for D3–D5 outside Valencia is **expected** under a tacit-knowledge technology even if transfer
  occurred, so D0 cannot discriminate "no transfer" from "unrecorded transfer."
- **Discriminative power lives in two places:** (a) feasible-zone non-adoption where cultivators were *not*
  retained (post-expulsion zones); (b) archaeology. Both are source-limited for this period.
- This is the audit's core epistemic finding: the period's surviving written record is structurally incapable
  of answering "did anyone try?" — it can only answer "was it institutionalized?" (D7) and "was the
  commodity consumed?" (D1).
