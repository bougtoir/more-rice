## Novelty assessment

**New primary evidence:** none — Phase 3's deepest finding is *meta-empirical* (the claimed flagship primary
exhibit, repartiment arrozales, is folio-unverified; Reg. 5 transcription is provably rice-free).

**New comparative architecture:** the D0–D7 staged diffusion ontology applied to a medieval crop across
three network layers (dynastic / administrative / practical) is, to our knowledge, not published for rice —
the closest analogues are crop-package studies (4) that lack the staged coding and the labor-margin contrast.

**New historically-grounded explanation:** "diffusion stopped at the practical layer, not the dynastic or
institutional layer; dependence was defended on the labor margin (1351/1388 statutes, verified text), not
the technology margin" — this formulation is original to this project.

**Honest verdict on Gate C (novelty):** MARGINAL-PASS. The contribution is a *reframing + negative audit +
comparative coding*, not new data. Suitable for a delimited article if framed as "an audit of what actually
diffused" — but it does not clear a high novelty bar (e.g., P&P, EREH flagship ambitions). Agricultural
History Review's scope (documentary agrarian history, incl. negative results) fits; the piece must lead
with the folio-verification failure as an honest finding, not bury it.
