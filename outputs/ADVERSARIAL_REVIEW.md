# ADVERSARIAL REVIEW — three hostile reviewers

## Reviewer 1 — Medieval economic historian

**Objection 1.1 — Serfdom anachronism.** "Your hypothesis treats 'the medieval peasant' as a uniformly coerced serf. Villeinage in 1300 England ≠ serfdom in 11th-c. Saxony ≠ free frontier settlers in Castile. H4's 'desire to preserve dependence' presumes a dependence that varied enormously and was *declining* in much of your window (commutation, communes, free frontier colonization)."

Supporting evidence: England commutation accelerated post-1180s; Castilian concejos granted fueros to attract settlers (U09); German east-Elbian coercion was only *beginning*.

Fatal? **Yes for H4's generality.** The hypothesis as stated cannot survive: there is no single "peasant dependence" regime to preserve. Surviving revision: restrict H4/H5 to *within-region* institutional comparison (U01 vs U05 vs U09).

**Objection 1.2 — Source authenticity.** "The one document that would place rice in early Sicily is a known Vella forgery (S06). Your whole 'Sicily cultivated rice under Arabs' storyline collapses; Falcando's silence (S07) is the real primary datum."

Fatal? **Yes for the Sicily sub-narrative** — corrected in this audit by excluding the forged rescript. This actually *strengthens* the negative result: one fewer claimed adoption site.

**Objection 1.3 — Documentary-survival bias.** "Absence of suppression ordinances proves nothing where record survival is thin (manorial memoranda, village by-laws)."

Fatal? Partially mitigated: the comparison includes high-survival corpora (English manorial accounts, Aragonese chancery registers) where ordinances *do* survive for other crop/land-use matters — the silence is patterned, not uniform. Residual limitation retained.

## Reviewer 2 — Agricultural historian

**Objection 2.1 — The 'upland rice' premise is anachronistic.** "Upland rice cultivars suited to temperate latitudes did not exist in the medieval varietal pool. What diffused through the Islamic world was essentially subtropical/tropical-adapted material grown *irrigated* in warm zones. You cannot infer 'upland rice could have grown in England' from 'rice exists as upland systems in modern Madagascar.'"

Supporting evidence: S16 upland trials show sterility at 13/19 °C; S17 crop norms; S04 archaeobotanical judgment.

Fatal? **Yes for the NW-European adoption counterfactual.** H1 survives only for the Mediterranean fringe — where rice *was* in fact adopted (al-Andalus, Valencia). So H1 collapses into a tautology: rice was feasible where it was grown.

**Objection 2.2 — Yield comparison is underpowered.** "You have no medieval rice yields; modern bounds (1–2 t/ha) are not medieval data."

Fatal? Not fatal to the *decision* — the comparison is run only as a bounded feasibility check and reports the uncertainty explicitly (Phase 3 spec). Medieval-productivity cells for rice are marked 'not documented'.

**Objection 2.3 — Processing/storage ignored.** "Rice needs dehusking; European milling infrastructure was built for wheat/rye/oats. Adoption would need a processing chain nobody describes."

Mitigant: adds a further non-political adoption barrier (lists with #5/#6 in COMPETING_EXPLANATIONS). Retained as limitation.

## Reviewer 3 — Political economist

**Objection 3.1 — The mechanism fails Domar logic.** "If landlords coerce because labor is scarce (Domar 1970), a *more* labor-intensive, higher-output crop makes coercion *more* profitable, not less. Suppression of a high-yield crop to 'preserve dependence' is non-identified and contradicts the profit motive you yourself document (Winchester lords raising yields, P06)."

Fatal? **Yes for H3/H4 as specified.** No rescue attempted; per spec, no supportive evidence was manufactured.

**Objection 3.2 — Endogeneity/selection.** "Iberian 'adoption under Christian rule' isn't a clean institutional contrast — the irrigation regime and mudéjar labor came with the territory; you cannot separate 'institutions' from 'inherited agronomy'."

Fatal? For causal claims: yes — hence the audit classifies Valencia as 'adoption continuity', not a treatment effect. For the negative conclusion: it *supports* it — adoption tracks environmental/infrastructural inheritance, not regime type.

**Objection 3.3 — Weak power of the intent audit.** "No contemporaneous document in Latin Europe even *discusses* rice cultivation policy — you cannot test H3 on an event that never entered the policy space."

Correct — and that is itself the finding: **rice cultivation never became a policy object in medieval Western Europe**, so H3 is unfalsifiable in the strong sense and unsupported in the weak sense. The honest decision is CONDITIONAL GO (comparative institutional study) or NO-GO for the rice-specific suppression claim.

## Disposition summary

| Hypothesis | Verdict after hostile review |
|------------|------------------------------|
| H1 feasibility | Partially supported — only in Mediterranean/irrigated fringe, which is also where adoption occurred |
| H2 knowledge | Supported narrowly: K1–K2 widespread; K3 confined to Arabic agronomy (no Latin transmission) |
| H3 political restriction | **Refined: restriction IS documented — but only in Valencia, for sanitary ('fiebres tercianas') and confessional ('Muslim food') motives, never dependence (P11–P13); the 1475 Milan intervention was promotion + export monopoly** |
| H4 dependence motive | **Not supported — no motive evidence; mechanism contradicts landlord incentives and Domar theory** |
| H5 institutional variation | Not testable on rice; negative controls (plough/rotation/mills/legumes) show innovation encouraged under high-coercion regimes → general suppression mechanism falsified |

**Preserved limitations:** (i) documentary-survival bias cannot be fully excluded; (ii) no medieval rice yield data exist — productivity comparison is bounded, not measured; (iii) within-Iberia institutional contrast is confounded by inherited agronomy; (iv) the audit relies on published editions, not fresh archival work.
