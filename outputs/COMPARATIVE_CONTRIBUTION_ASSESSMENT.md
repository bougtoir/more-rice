# COMPARATIVE CONTRIBUTION ASSESSMENT (Task 5)

## Correction of the previous formulation

"Rice never entered the medieval Western European policy space" is **withdrawn**.
It conflicts with the Llibre del Repartiment (rice fields registered 1238–1252),
the Furs' irrigation regime, and the Valencian sanitary restrictions — rice was
a policy object in Iberia, both protected and periodically restricted.

## Differentiated historical pattern (replaces single narrative)

| Pattern class | Regions | Status |

## Five-factor interaction — where it operated

| Factor | Where demonstrably operative | Where absent/not identifiable |
|---|---|---|
| Environmental suitability | everywhere (necessary but insufficient alone) | — |
| Practical knowledge | only via conquest/cultivator migration | NW Europe: no surviving evidence |
| Irrigation & land-use institutions | Valencia (continuity, offices, water law) | absent or untested elsewhere |
| Economic incentives | Valencia (revenue continuity), Milan 1475 (ducal monopoly) | NW Europe: K1 only, no cultivation market |
| Political/legal arrangements | Valencia (both protect and restrict) | no crop-choice regulation found anywhere |

The five factors are **not jointly necessary** — only environment + knowledge +
a delivery channel appear together in every adoption case; they are not shown
sufficient anywhere (Valencia's adoption also needed the inherited
infrastructure). The typology is **descriptive, not a causal identification
strategy** — stated explicitly to avoid overreach.

## Answer to the central question

Can the evidence support a historically *original explanation* of uneven
diffusion? **Yes, in qualified form**: "adoption occurred only where
environment, surviving cultivator populations, and institutional continuity
coincided; its failures are differentiated (climatic where the climate is
impossible, epistemic where it is merely marginal, infrastructural where water
institutions were absent) — and no documented case attributes failure to a
political motive." This is an explanation of *differential failure*, new as a
structured claim though built entirely from published evidence.
