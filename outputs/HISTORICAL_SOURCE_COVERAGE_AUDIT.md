## Honest coverage verdict

- **No archival research was performed.** Coverage is via published editions and
  scholarly syntheses; the audit cannot claim exhaustiveness for Western Europe.
- The correct formulation throughout is therefore:
  *"No suppression documents were identified in the searched materials"* —
  not "no suppression documents existed."
- **Patterned silence argument (weakest leg, kept limited):** in the corpora
  where regulation documents *do* survive (Aragonese chancery output; English
  statutes and manorial accounts), rice regulation does not appear where it
  would be expected if rice were a policy object — but rice may never have been
  a policy object at all, making the silence uninformative rather than probative.
- **Comparison with other agricultural regulations:** crop-relevant regulation
  survives for mills (banalités), gleaning, open-field rules, tithes — none of
  it targets crop choice toward or away from any cereal. The silence about rice
  is consistent with the general absence of crop-choice regulation.
- **Known losses/biases:** early Capetian and English local by-laws survive
  thinly pre-1250; mudéjar community records largely lost (inquisitorial
  destruction); Orihuela repartimiento silence may reflect registration
  practice rather than actual cultivation.
- **Sufficiency for a negative finding:** adequate to state "unsupported by
  available evidence" (the revised H3 verdict); *inadequate* for "suppression
  never occurred."
