# KNOWLEDGE TRANSMISSION REASSESSMENT (Task 3)

## Corrected scale (K0–K5)

K0 none · K1 commodity · K2 cultivation-known-elsewhere · K3 practical methods ·
K4 access to seed & experienced cultivators · K5 demonstrated local capability.

## Channel-by-channel assessment

| Channel | Evidence found | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Written translation (Arabic→Latin) | No medieval Latin translation of any Filāḥa treatise identified; Ibn al-Awwam first translated 1802 | **Demonstrated absence of a textual channel** — but only for manuals |
| Oral/migration (mudéjar communities) | Conquered Muslim agricultural populations remained and farmed under cartas puebla; irrigation expertise demonstrably transferred (Valencia water law preserves Andalusi practice) | **Demonstrated transmission within Iberia** (K3–K5 locally) |
| Merchant networks | Hanseatic/Levant trade carried rice as commodity (K1), not cultivation skill | Demonstrated K1 only |
| Monastic networks | No record of monastic rice trials; Cistercian agronomy was innovative in drainage/milling, not exotic crops | No surviving evidence of transmission |
| Cross-cultural irrigation communities | Valencia: institutional knowledge demonstrably crossed the conquest via persons (cequieros, mudéjar farmers), not texts | **Demonstrated transmission — but only across the conquest frontier inside Iberia** |
| Diplomatic exchange | Sforza→Este 1475 seed diplomacy — post-period, proves seed+advice could travel as state gift | Demonstrated (post-period) mechanism that no medieval document attests north of Pyrenees |
| Skilled agricultural labor | Mudéjar/ morisco cultivators = the actual K4 vector; no evidence any such cultivators reached NW Europe | No surviving evidence of transmission |

## Revised verdicts per region

| Region | Previous claim | Revised claim |
|---|---|---|
| Islamic Iberia | K3–K4 | K3–K5 (unchanged) |
| Christian Valencia | K3–K4 via conquest | **K5 demonstrated** — rice fields registered in Llibre del Repartiment; transmission mechanism = institutional + human, not textual |
| England / N. France / Germany | "K1 at best, K3 absent" | **K1 demonstrated; K2 plausible; K3–K4: no surviving evidence of transmission.** Corrected from "no transmission" to "no surviving evidence" — oral/migrant channels cannot be excluded by the absence of a Latin manual |
| Mediterranean France | K1–K2 | K1–K2 demonstrated; K3: uncertain (trade proximity to Iberia) |

## Net correction

- The claim "practical knowledge did not enter Latin decision-making circles" is
  retained for the *textual* channel and weakened to **"no surviving evidence"**
  overall. It remains true that no document attests K3+ knowledge reaching NW
  European rulers; it is no longer claimed that such knowledge demonstrably
  never traveled.
- The dominant transmission mode identified is **human and institutional**
  (conquered cultivators, inherited water offices), which explains the observed
  pattern better than texts ever could: knowledge moved only where Muslim
  cultivators moved.
