## Negative-controls reassessment

Prior claim: plough/rotation/mills/legumes "spread WITH elite encouragement,"
falsifying a general suppression mechanism. Reassessed:

- Diffusion of the heavy plough and three-field system occurred largely
  *through* peasant communities and manorial routine; explicit ruler/landlord
  *promotion* documents are rare. Water mills were often seigneurial
  investments (banalité revenues) — genuinely lord-encouraged. Legumes and
  drainage: mixed, estate-level decisions.
- Corrected statement: "productivity-enhancing innovations were **adopted
  within** strong-coercion societies, several with documented landlord
  investment (mills, drainage)." This weakens — does not falsify — a *general*
  suppression mechanism, and says nothing about *selective* suppression of a
  single novel crop.
- H5 therefore: not identifiable from this evidence.

## What now stands as the strongest defensible statement

> No document in the searched materials records suppression of rice for any
> motive; the only documented rice restrictions (Valencia) were sanitary and
> confessional; the only documented promotion was explicit (Iberian continuity,
> Sforza). The suppression hypothesis is **unsupported by the available
> evidence and inconsistent with the observed direction of all documented
> interventions** — levels 2–3, not 5.

This preserves the NO-GO for the affirmative hypothesis without overstating it
as falsification.
