| Germany pass share | **96.57%** | **3.23%** |

Both endpoints are reproduced exactly (manuscript values 96.6% and 3.2%). The surface, window, mask, weights and resolution are identical, so **the entire contrast lies in the threshold**, i.e. in how the Hokkaido reference was constructed. The two references are different estimands: a cold-tail quantile of a rice-area distribution on a 1970-2000 grid versus a single-point daily value for 2000-2024.

The path changes one factor at a time. Because the German share is a steep, non-linear function of the threshold (most German incumbent arable land lies between about 600 and 900 GDD10), the pass-share change attributed to a step depends on where on the path it is applied; the table is one admissible ordering, not a unique additive decomposition. The threshold changes, by contrast, are order-independent for steps 1-3 (they alter only the point reference) and for steps 4-5 (they alter only the grid reference).

**Interpretation.** The quantile choice (step 5) is the largest single component (-270 GDD10; +88 pp). Steps 1-3 (period, temporal aggregation, data source/support) together lower the point reference by -153 GDD10. Step 4 shows that the single POWER point is not representative of Hokkaido rice: it lies in Tokachi, with 5,042 ha of Hokkaido's 113,968 ha of SPAM rice within ±0.5°, and the WorldClim cell there is colder than the rice-area median.

## 6. Interactions

* Window × reference: the window effect on the German share is -10.83 pp for T* and -2.34 pp for the POWER point; the difference arises because the two thresholds sit at different positions on the German GDD distribution (non-linearity), not because of an independent window mechanism.
* Resolution × window: recalibrated-resolution range 4.2 pp (pipeline) vs 17.9 pp (Jun–Sep).
* Resolution × reference: range 4.2 pp at recalibrated T* vs 0.55 pp at a fixed 900.
* A full factorial over data source × reference construction is not defined, because the grid-quantile reference has no point-data counterpart (POWER provides no gridded Hokkaido rice-area distribution at 5′) and POWER does not cover 1970-1980.

## 8. Conclusions for the manuscript

1. Threshold contribution: the contrast is entirely a threshold contrast on an identical surface.
2. Temporal-window contribution: changes the T* endpoint by -10.8 pp; secondary.
3. Dataset/reference-construction contribution: dominant (quantile +88 pp at the end of the path; period, aggregation and source together lower the point reference by 153 GDD10; single-point location spans 0.9–33.3%).
4. Spatial-support contribution: ≤2.4 pp (primary window); larger only near the threshold in the Jun–Sep window.
5. Remaining confounding: POWER 1981-2000 vs WorldClim 1970-2000 differ by one decade; POWER cell vs WorldClim cell differ in both source and support (not separable with available data); SPAM 2010 weights are fixed.
6. The original headline "97% vs 3% shows dependence on data and spatial scale" is **not defensible as a scale effect**. It is defensible as a contrast between two reference constructions (estimands).
7. Manuscript wording adopted: "This contrast is not a resolution effect … The contrast is therefore mainly a contrast between estimands: a cold-tail bound of the Hokkaido rice distribution versus a single-point value near its centre, measured in a warmer period with daily data." (Section 3.3; Methods 2.5; Figure 3d; abstract and highlights revised accordingly.)
