Title: Where could rice cultivation systems be transferred beyond traditional growing regions? A multi-scale gate analysis of thermal transfer, water access and food-supply risk on European arable land

Article type: Research article

Abstract (247 words):
Proposals to grow rice outside its traditional regions often treat a climatic threshold as evidence of suitability. We test that inference geographically for 69.7 Mha of incumbent arable land in Europe (SPAM 2010 harvested area of wheat, maize, barley and potato, 5′ grid) using five sequential, non-compensatory gates: thermal transfer of a cold-region japonica system, irrigation infrastructure, rainfed water balance, economic parity and food-supply risk. Calibrating the Hokkaido cold bound on the same WorldClim grid gives 589 GDD10 (5th percentile of Hokkaido rice area), not the ~900 GDD10 obtained from NASA POWER point cells; the thermal gate is therefore met by 92.2% of incumbent arable land, whereas a transplanted 900 threshold would retain 54.9% and almost exclude Germany (96.6% vs 3.2%). Water, not temperature, is the binding constraint: 16.0% of incumbent arable land combines thermal compatibility with irrigation infrastructure (area equipped ≥10%), while 76.0% faces a positive Jun–Sep rainfed deficit. Point-scale daily water balances agree with the grid deficit (Spearman ρ = 0.73). On the Lower Danube, a 250,000 ha paddy scenario would require ~241 m³/s, about 8.8% of median observed summer low flow; allocation, not discharge, is unresolved. Correcting an analog-yield inconsistency and adding block-bootstrap intervals, modeled shortfall-probability reductions have bootstrap intervals that exclude zero in 58% of sensitivity configurations for Kazakhstan and 17% for Romania (baseline 95% CI −0.156 to 0.000), and are not robust elsewhere. The results identify a small set of irrigated-infrastructure regions for next-stage feasibility assessment, not land allocations or implemented irrigation.
