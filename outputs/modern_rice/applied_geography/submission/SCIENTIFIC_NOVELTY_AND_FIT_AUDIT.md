# Scientific novelty and fit audit (G1, G2)

## Fit with Applied Geography's scope
- The scope covers resource assessment and allocation problems with a spatial dimension. The revised manuscript is built around such a problem: where existing arable land is exposed to each gate for transferring a cultivation system. Its spatial unit is explicit (5′ cells), as is its denominator (incumbent arable harvested area). It also tests sensitivity to scale and aggregation (MAUP).
- 12 of the 19 rows in the prior-work matrix are Applied Geography papers on land suitability, cold-margin rice and irrigation-related land use, so the reference frame matches the journal.
- **Residual fit risk (medium).** The portfolio-risk component is agronomic and economic. It is kept as a downstream gate (G5) and is not the main result.

## Changes to the scientific conclusion compared with the Agricultural Systems version
1. "Hokkaido ~900 GDD10 reclassifies Rhine/Alsace/Loire/Berlin/Saxony as transfer candidates" is replaced. Thermal compatibility is widespread; the limiting factor is the lack of infrastructure.
2. "Lower Danube demand ~24 m³/s, ~2% of Q99" was **wrong**. The correct figure is about 241 m³/s, 21.9% of Q99.
3. The Serbia rice/potato contrast was an implementation artifact and has been removed.
4. Romania's risk reduction is no longer reported as clear-cut: its CI includes 0.

## Verdict
G1 (scientific validity) passes after these corrections. G2 (novelty) passes conditionally: the contribution is methodological and geographic, and modest in size. A desk rejection on grounds of limited novelty remains possible.
