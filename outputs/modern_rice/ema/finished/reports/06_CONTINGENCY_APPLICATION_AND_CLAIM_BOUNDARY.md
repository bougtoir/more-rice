## Text as built

> Beyond long-term agricultural adaptation, spatial suitability screening could also provide a preliminary input to contingency crop-substitution planning after a widespread crop-specific disease outbreak; a severe disruption to wheat production, for example, could prompt an assessment of alternative crops. This study did not evaluate any disease scenario, and its results do not identify rice as a preferred alternative. They do indicate why thermal screening alone would be insufficient for such planning: the candidate area depends strongly on reference construction, and mapped irrigation infrastructure, which is not evidence of available water, imposes a further constraint. Operational substitution capacity would require separate assessment of water availability, seed supply, machinery, seasonal timing, conversion costs and attainable yields.
