# Hostile EM&A editorial assessment

This simulates a handling editor at Environmental Monitoring and Assessment reading the AG manuscript as it stands, and after the minimum changes in `EMA_MINIMAL_CHANGE_FEASIBILITY.md`. Scope quotes come from `data/journal_requirements/environmental_monitoring_and_assessment/aims_and_scope.txt` (accessed 2026-11-13).

1. **Why might an editor send it for review?** It covers a continental domain (69.7 Mha), uses only open observational datasets, and includes an out-of-sample check: 0.87% of the 501,075 ha of observed European rice fails the Hokkaido-derived gate. It also delivers a quantified warning relevant to anyone who transfers a climatic threshold. EM&A regularly publishes crop-suitability work (19 audited analogues), and this paper is broader and more self-critical than most of them.
2. **Why desk-reject it?** "Preference is given to studies with new and original environmental field data collected and analyzed by the authors". The paper has none. Its framing is geographic (it was written for Applied Geography), it cites no EM&A work, and it contains an economic-parity section, whereas "Economic based studies" are listed as generally out of scope.
3. **Is the environmental-assessment contribution explicit?** Not in the AG version: the question is posed as a geographic transfer question. It could be made explicit (reliability of a threshold-transfer suitability assessment) without changing any result.
4. **Is it more than another GIS suitability map?** Yes, if the decomposition leads the paper. No audited EM&A paper separates reference construction (point vs grid, period, temporal formula, quantile: 903 → 589 GDD10) from spatial support (≤2.4 points). If the paper leads with the gate cascade map, it reads like the analogues.
5. **Are the sensitivity analyses consequential?** One is: the same surface yields 96.6% or 3.2% thermal pass for Germany. The others (window, domain, AEI threshold) are routine and should be reported briefly.
6. **Is external validation insufficient?** Partly. Omission against observed rice is tested out of sample. Commission cannot be tested, because the gate passes 92.2% of land, and there are no yield or adoption outcomes. The analogues that validate against field yield (Mohamed 2019; Azadi 2023; Vahidi 2023) set a higher bar on this point.
7. **Is the study primarily geographic rather than environmental?** In its current form, yes. Its environmental-assessment content lies in the irrigation-infrastructure and water-demand gates (Lower Danube net 241.1 m³/s, gross 321.5 m³/s), but it explicitly does not assess environmental impact on water bodies. River discharge is not treated as allocable water.
8. **Would EM&A readers be interested?** Readers who build suitability or monitoring classifications from transferred thresholds would be. Readers interested in pollution or field monitoring would not.
9. **Strongest argument for publication.** It shows with real data that the choice of reference construction, which is rarely reported, can flip an assessment outcome far more than the resolution choice that assessments usually worry about. It does so at continental scale and with an out-of-sample omission check.
10. **Strongest argument against publication.** It is a desk-based modelling screen with no field data of the authors' own and no commission validation, about crop transfer (agricultural) rather than environmental condition. EM&A states a preference against exactly this profile.

**Editorial estimate:** the AG version is likely to be desk-rejected at EM&A. The reframed version, with economics moved to supplementary material, would plausibly be sent to review, with a residual risk from the field-data preference. This is a judgement, not an observed outcome.
