## Boundary statement

What can honestly be claimed: a quantified, decomposed demonstration that transferring a source-region thermal bound is dominated by reference construction rather than grid resolution, and that infrastructure rather than temperature limits a European rice-transfer screen. These are a novel empirical finding plus a methodological caution, within a context-specific application.

What cannot be claimed: a new method, a validated assessment system, observed land-use change, implementation feasibility, or monitoring data.

Existing validation evidence (already in the AG manuscript, section 2.4; `outputs/modern_rice/applied_geography/EUROPE_RICE_VALIDATION.json`): of 501,075 ha of SPAM-2010 rice in Europe, 4,346 ha (0.87%) fail the thermal gate and 406,209 ha (81.1%) lie in cells with irrigation infrastructure. T* was calibrated on Hokkaido, so European rice cells are out of sample for the threshold. This is an omission (sensitivity) check. It cannot measure commission, because the gate passes 92.2% of incumbent arable land.

Consequence for journal choice: the strongest elements (1 and 5) are judged on novelty, which AG found insufficient for a geography readership. The audited EM&A record (`EMA_CLOSEST_PUBLISHED_PAPERS.csv`) shows EM&A publishing crop-suitability applications whose novelty is mostly context-specific: 17/19 introduce no new method (one claims a new spatial model and one is a partly new ANP-fuzzy application), and 8/19 report no new field observations in the abstract. Against that record, contributions 1 and 5 are above the typical novelty of EM&A suitability papers. EM&A's barrier is therefore its scope and data preferences (its own field data, no economics, monitoring relevance) rather than novelty.
